Beyond Execution: Uncertainty Estimation and Abstention in LLM-Based Code Generation

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly adopted for automated code generation, assisting developers in writing programs from natural language descriptions. However, these models often produce outputs that appear plausible but contain subtle errors, posing risks to software correctness and reliability. Existing validation methods, such as execution-based testing and program analysis tools, have not been successful due to a fundamental limitation: the lack of groundtruth, which prevents proactive assessment of whether a model output is likely to fail.
An alternative strategy is to assess the model’s internal signals to estimate the likelihood of failure before relying on post-hoc validation. In this work, we aim to systematically investigate uncertainty estimation for code generation by comparing different types of uncertainty signals, evaluating their effectiveness across models and tasks, and examining how signal timing—such as during prefill versus decoding—impacts predictive reliability. We evaluate 16 uncertainty metrics across four benchmarks and two models, covering 128 experimental settings. Our evaluation spans both misprediction detection and abstention strategies.
Our results highlight that information-theoretic metrics, such as sequence likelihood, consistently outperform more complex alternatives for identifying mispredictions. However, calibration remains weak, and no single metric generalizes across all settings. We further find that uncertainty measured during decoding provides more reliable signals than prefill uncertainty, underscoring the importance of modeling uncertainty over generated outputs. These findings reveal both the potential and the current limitations of uncertaintybased methods for improving the robustness of AI-assisted coding tools. Based on our results, we provide recommendations for future research to advance uncertainty-aware techniques for reliable code generation.
